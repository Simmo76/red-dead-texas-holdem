# Cinematic Poker — Go-to-Market & $1,000 Launch Marketing Plan

*Scope: maximise exposure and revenue for the iOS/Android launch with a US$1,000 paid budget, using the product we actually have: offline single-player Texas Hold'em with believable NPCs, probabilistic tells, cinematic environments, a coaching mode, and a zero-install web demo.*

---

## 1. The one-sentence positioning

> **"The poker game that respects you: no chip store, no ads, no internet — just you against six characters who bluff, tilt and remember how you play."**

Everything below flows from this. The dominant mobile poker apps (Zynga Poker, WSOP, PokerStars Play, Governor of Poker 3) are **online chip-economy casinos**: they sell virtual chips, interrupt with ads, require a connection, and their "opponents" are other grinders or thin bots. Players *loudly* resent this — read any app-store review thread. We are the counter-position, and counter-positioning is the only marketing strategy that works on $1,000.

## 2. Competitive landscape and our edge

| Competitor | Model | Weakness we exploit |
|---|---|---|
| **Zynga Poker, WSOP (Playtika), PokerStars Play** | F2P chip economy, online multiplayer | Predatory chip sales, ads, connection required, zero personality at the table |
| **Governor of Poker 3** | F2P chip economy with a campaign veneer | Abandoned the beloved paid single-player formula of GoP 1–2; fans say so in reviews |
| **Governor of Poker 1–2** | Paid single-player | Closest true competitor — but 10+ years old, dated presentation, simplistic AI, no tells/coaching |
| **Poker Night at the Inventory 1 & 2 (Telltale, PC)** | Premium, character-driven | **Delisted and unavailable.** Proof there is real demand for personality-driven single-player poker — and nobody is serving it on mobile |
| **Prominence Poker** | F2P, console/PC, online | Not mobile, not offline |
| **Balatro** (adjacent) | Premium card game | Not a competitor, but the playbook: a premium card game exploding through streamers/creators proves the channel we should use |

**Our defensible edges (real features, not claims):**

1. **Opponents with character.** 12-parameter AI personalities, tilt model, opponent memory (VPIP/PFR/3-bet tracking), probabilistic tells with false tells. Nobody on mobile has this.
2. **Offline-first.** Flights, commutes, no account, no server. A genuinely under-served use case ("offline poker" is a high-intent, low-competition search term).
3. **Ethical monetisation.** We sell *places*, never chips. This is both a moral stance and a marketing hook.
4. **"Improve My Game" coaching.** Hand-history review turns the game into a poker trainer — a second audience (people who want to get better at real poker) and a second keyword cluster.
5. **The web demo.** A shareable, zero-install, playable link running the identical engine. This is a marketing weapon most indie mobile games don't have: every post, comment and email can end in "play it right now in your browser."

## 3. Paid or free-to-play?

**Recommendation: free to download, premium DLC — not paid up-front, and emphatically not a chip economy.**

- **Free base game:** Mates' Kitchen (and the full poker engine, all five NPCs, coaching) free forever. No ads. No energy. No chips for sale, ever.
- **Paid content:** environment packs at **$3.99–4.99** each (new setting + new NPC cast + new tells/dialogue), plus a **"Everything Edition" one-time unlock at $14.99–19.99** covering all current and future environments. Price the bundle so it's the obvious choice for anyone who buys two packs.
- Each pack's table is **visitable but not playable** from the free game (walk in, watch a hand, meet the characters) so the store is discovered through play, not through a paywall gate.

Why not paid up-front ($4.99): a paid app with a $1,000 budget and no publisher gets a few hundred downloads and dies in store rankings. Free removes the only friction we can't argue against, lets the web demo → store funnel convert at maximum rate, and the "free, and we will never sell you chips" message is itself the viral hook. Why not subscriptions or ads: they destroy the positioning in §1, which is worth more than any short-term revenue.

Secondary recommendation: on top of packs, a single **$2.99 tip-jar/supporter IAP** ("buy the dealer a beer") — costs nothing to add, and games with loyal small audiences see meaningful revenue from it.

## 4. Business strategy

1. **Phase gate first.** Do not spend a dollar of paid budget until the Phase 3 vertical-slice bar is met: *five NPCs at Mates' Kitchen stay entertaining for 15–30 minutes.* Marketing a product that fails its own fun test burns the budget and the audience.
2. **Build in public (free, starts now).** Weekly short-form devlogs (TikTok / YouTube Shorts / Reels): "I'm building the poker game Zynga won't", NPC tell showcases, "my AI went on tilt" clips. Character-and-AI content is inherently clippable. Every clip links to the web demo.
3. **Web demo as top of funnel.** Add light analytics (hands played, session length) and a "Get it on iOS/Android" banner with UTM links. Share the link in every Reddit comment, every creator email, every post. Zero friction = maximum conversion.
4. **ASO before ads.** Title/subtitle targeting the under-served cluster: *offline poker*, *poker vs AI*, *single player poker*, *Texas Hold'em offline*, *poker trainer*. Screenshots lead with characters' faces and tells, not a top-down table (every competitor shows a table; nobody shows a *person*).
5. **Launch sequencing:** soft-launch on Android (cheaper installs, faster review cycles) → fix retention → global launch both stores in the same week as the biggest creator videos (coordinated spike maximises store-ranking algorithms) → one environment pack per 6–8 weeks, each pack release is a fresh marketing beat with its own trailer and creator wave.
6. **Second audience: poker learners.** The coaching feature lets us market in "learn poker" spaces (r/poker, poker-training YouTube/TikTok) where game ads never appear. "Practise reading tells without losing real money" is a unique message no competitor can copy.
7. **Revenue maths to sanity-check against:** at a realistic 2–4% payer conversion and ~$8 average revenue per paying user (one pack + some bundles), 10,000 installs ≈ $1,600–3,200. The $1,000 budget's job is not profit — it's to find a repeatable channel where cost per install < $0.50 and D7 retention > 10%, which is the evidence needed to justify spending (or raising) real money later.

**Deliberately $0:** Facebook/Google broad UA (minimum viable budgets are 10–50× ours), press agencies, paid reviews, app-store feature brokers. **Free actions that matter more than any line above:** devlogs, Reddit/Discord participation as a developer (not an advertiser), a press kit page, submitting to free indie showcases and "best offline games" listicle authors (pitch them directly — those articles drive durable installs for years).

## 6. Measurement

- **UTM-tag every link**; web demo and store listings are the two funnels.
- Weekly dashboard: installs by source, CPI, D1/D7 retention, median session length, % reaching hand 10, payer conversion, pack attach rate.
- **Kill rule:** any paid channel that can't beat $1.50 CPI with D7 > 10% after $100 spent is stopped. The budget is 10 experiments, not one campaign.

## 7. Risks

- **Fun bar not met** → gate all spend on the Phase 3 slice (see §4.1).
- **"Poker" ad-policy friction** (platforms sometimes flag poker as gambling) → creatives emphasise *no real money, no chips for sale*; have store/ad reviewers' simulated-gambling categories filled in correctly from day one.
- **Soft web-demo cannibalisation** → acceptable; the demo converts sceptics the store page never would. Keep one environment store-exclusive if data shows a problem.
